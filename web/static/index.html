What she does

Nineteen things, and that is the list rather than a sample.

She routes a sentence to one of them or says she cannot. There is no twentieth thing she will attempt badly.

Understand it

  • what does my agent actually do, step by step?
  • what conversation history does this node get?
  • what does this node remember between calls?
  • what tools is this node offered, and which does it actually call?
  • what skills are bound at this call site?
  • how many turns does it take, and in what loop?
  • what is this node allowed to do — what can it reach, and what can it spend?

Judge it

  • look at my repository and tell me what is weak
  • build me an evaluation set for this agent
  • is this version better than the last one?
  • what did you measure, and what did you not?
  • what happened in that run?
  • should these nodes run in this order?

Change it

  • change what this node is told
  • change which model this node calls
  • change something on an axis and show me the diff
  • what exactly would you write into my source?
  • fix it, and open a pull request
  • put an approved change into my repository

Who she is

She would rather refuse than guess.

“I cannot route that. Here is the whole of what I do — nineteen things, and this is the list rather than a sample.”when a sentence is outside what she does

“The plan is written down before anything is spent.”before every run

“Give the run a limit — a number of proposals, a spend, or a deadline.” asked to keep improving until it is perfect

Most assistants guess when they are unsure, because guessing looks like helpfulness. The cost of the two mistakes is not symmetric. An abstention costs one exchange. A wrong route spends your money on work you did not ask for, and a conversation is exactly where you are least equipped to notice you were sent somewhere else.

So she routes deterministically, publishes the whole list of what she can do, and says nothing was spent when nothing was. Every refusal names the next action, because “no” without a next step is just a door.

When she does run, the plan and its ceiling are written down first, every task reports what it cost, and an effect on your repository waits for you to approve it.

Getting started

Three steps, and the first one is free.

1 · Create an organization

An address and a password. You own it.

2 · Give her a repository

Paste a GitHub link. She says plainly when there is no agent in it to assess.

3 · Ask

In a sentence. She routes it, or tells you she cannot and shows you the list.
