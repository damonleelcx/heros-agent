This notice describes what the Heros platform, the `heros` command-line tool and the web console
(together, the **Service**) do with data, and who does it. It is written by **PLUTUX TECHNOLOGY LLC**,
930 S 4th St, Ste 209 #281, Las Vegas, Nevada 89101, United States.

It was written against an inventory of the system's actual stores, taken from the database migration
chain rather than from memory. Where the system does not do something, this notice says so instead of
leaving a silence — and where a right has no route we have built, it says that too.

## 1. The fact that shapes everything below

**One deployment serves one tenant boundary.** Isolation between customers is at the level of the
deployment, not a shared multi-tenant database.

**There is no vendor-operated hosted deployment today.** Every deployment of this Service in existence is
run by a customer or by an operator on a customer's behalf, including the air-gapped package that has no
outbound network access at all.

The consequence is direct and it is not a formality: **for a deployment you or your operator run, PLUTUX
is not a processor of the data in sections 3 and 4** — that data never reaches us. The operator of the
deployment is. If that changes, this notice changes with it, and that change will be recorded as
**material**, which means you will be asked to accept it before your next commitment.

## 2. What we collect when you deal with us directly

If you sign in to a console we operate, or purchase a plan:

| Data | Why | Kept |
|---|---|---|
| An opaque tenant identifier and an opaque principal identifier | To bind a session and an entitlement to an account. **Never an email address, never a name** | For the life of the account |
| A session record — issue, expiry, revocation | To keep you signed in, and to end it | **8 hours**, and a console restart ends every session |
| An identity-provider subject, where you sign in through your own identity provider | To recognise you on return. It is the opaque subject your provider issues — **not an email, not a name** | For the life of the account |
| An opaque billing-provider handle, plan, and usage quantities | To bill you correctly | For the life of the account and any statutory period |
| A record of your acceptance of a legal document | To answer "what exactly did this customer accept, and when" | **7 years** — see section 6 |

**We do not receive, hold or store card details.** Payment is handled entirely by Stripe. A database
constraint rejects card-shaped data outright, so card data cannot enter our systems even through a
mis-wired integration.

Your browser holds **one cookie**: an opaque session token it cannot read, marked `HttpOnly`. It is
strictly necessary to keep you signed in, which is why there is no cookie banner — there is nothing
optional to consent to.

## 3. What the Service stores about your work

These are the stores that hold data derived from your repositories and your runs. They live **inside the
deployment**, wherever that is.

| Store | What is in it |
|---|---|
| Discovery and configuration | Repository URL, commit, language, the workflow graph, configuration identities and lineage |
| Registries | Model, prompt, skill and context entries. Prompt bodies are stored by content hash in the object store |
| Transforms | Variant specifications, diffs, build status, and the build log your own toolchain produced |
| Runs and execution | Run lifecycle, per-node execution records, timings and costs |
| Evaluation and attribution | Scores, confidence intervals, gate verdicts, coverage, attributions, proposals and verdicts |
| Delivery | The repository and pull request a change was delivered to, and the state it reached |
| Object store | **Prompt text, completion text, diffs and reports**, addressed by content hash. This is the most sensitive store the Service has |
| Telemetry | Traces and metrics for your runs |

Two facts about telemetry are worth stating precisely, because they are enforced in one place rather than
promised in many:

- **Every event and every span passes through a single scrubbing stage** before it reaches any store. It
  removes secrets, API keys, prompt text, completion text and personal data, replacing substantial content
  with a content-hash reference. So prompt and completion text do not reach a log, a trace or a metric —
  telemetry carries a pointer, never the words.
- **Your model-provider keys are never stored anywhere in this Service.** They are read on the machine
  running the tool, at the moment a model is called.

## 4. Retention, stated as it actually is

This is the section a privacy notice most often overstates, so it is written plainly.

| Data | Retention |
|---|---|
| Console sessions | **8 hours**, enforced. A restart ends every session |
| Records of your acceptance of a legal document | **7 years** |
| Telemetry traces and metrics | Whatever the operator of the deployment has configured on their tracing and metrics backends |
| **Everything else in section 3** | **No automatic deletion.** It persists until the operator removes it, or until an erasure request is executed |

We are not going to tell you that eval results, delivery records or stored prompts are deleted after
some number of days, because **no job in this Service deletes them**. Plan limits include a retention
allowance, and today that allowance is a stated figure rather than an enforced deletion. Saying otherwise
would describe a mechanism that does not exist.

## 5. Who else sees data

Each of the following is a third party **you or your operator bring**, not a vendor we have placed in the
path.

| System | What reaches it |
|---|---|
| **Your model providers** | Your prompts and completions, on your own provider keys and your own provider account |
| **Stripe** | Billing identifiers, subscription and usage quantities. Card details go **directly** to Stripe and never through us |
| **Your identity provider** | An authentication request, when you choose to sign in through it |
| **Your forge** | In the default delivery path, **nothing from us** — your own CI opens the pull request with its own credential. We store only the pull request's citation |

**This notice names no sub-processors, because there are none to name.** There is no vendor-operated
hosted deployment for a sub-processor to serve. Where you operate the deployment, the systems it talks to
are the ones you configured, and disclosing them is yours to do.

If your operator configures telemetry export to a third-party service, that is a transfer this notice
cannot see. Ask your operator what they export and where.

## 6. Records of your acceptance

When you accept a legal document, we record exactly six things:

- an opaque tenant identifier and an opaque principal identifier;
- which document, which version, and the **content hash** of the exact text you were shown;
- when, and by which route (sign-in, checkout, plan change, or the API).

**No email address. No name. No free text. No IP address. No user agent.** The record is deliberately
minimal, and that minimalism is what allows the next paragraph to be true.

The record is **append-only**: a re-acceptance or a withdrawal is a new row, and nothing is rewritten.
Duplicate submissions collapse to one row through a database constraint rather than through application
logic, so a double-clicked button cannot produce two records of one decision.

Retention is **7 years**. Deletion runs against a configured window and can be run in dry mode; where no
window is configured, the job deletes nothing rather than assuming one.

## 7. Your rights, and the route that actually exists

Depending on where you live you may have rights to access, correct, delete, or receive a copy of your
personal data, and to object to or restrict certain processing.

**We assert only rights we can actually execute, and only by the route that exists.**

That route is: **write to us at the address in section 10.** An operator receives the request, records it
against an auditable request record, verifies it, and executes it against the deployment.

There is **no self-serve export button and no self-serve delete button** in the console today. Saying
otherwise would be more comfortable to read and would send you looking for a control that is not there.

### What erasure does, precisely

Erasure **tombstones the subject** and **keeps the evidentiary rows**. Specifically:

- Identifiers that point at you are removed or replaced with a non-personal reference.
- A record of your acceptance of a legal document is **kept** — the document, the version, the hash and
  the timestamp. It contains no personal data beyond an opaque identifier, so keeping it discloses nothing
  about you while preserving the answer to "what was agreed".
- **No entry is removed from the audit chain.** That chain refuses updates and deletions at the database
  level, for every role including the highest, which is what makes it worth anything as an audit chain.

If you need every trace of an identity gone including its evidence, tell us, and we will tell you plainly
what we can and cannot do rather than agreeing and then not doing it.

## 8. Security, described rather than claimed

What is enforced by mechanism, and therefore worth stating:

- Card data cannot enter the system — a database constraint refuses it.
- Prompt and completion text cannot reach a span, a metric or a log — one scrubbing stage, on every event.
- The console never receives a platform credential in the browser; the build fails if credential material
  reaches a shipped browser chunk.
- Operator access to a customer's data is recorded, with the reason and the scope, in an append-only
  audit chain that cannot be edited or deleted by anyone.
- In the default delivery path, no credential to your repository exists on our side to be stolen.

**We hold no security certification.** No SOC 2 report, no ISO certification, no audit attestation. We
would rather you learn that here than in a questionnaire.

## 9. Changes to this notice

Every version we publish stays at its own permanent address. The [version history](/legal) lists each one
with its effective date, whether the change is **material**, and a content hash of the exact text — so you
can confirm that the text you read is the text a record refers to.

A material change asks for your acceptance at your next commitment. It never blocks you from reading the
console, from a run already in progress, or from this document.

- **It does not offer a self-serve export or deletion control**, because none exists (section 7).

Each of these is a gap we can see. When one closes, it closes in a new version of this notice, recorded
as material — not quietly.
