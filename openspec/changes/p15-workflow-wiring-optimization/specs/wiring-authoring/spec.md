# Wiring Authoring — Spec Delta (P15)

Product rationale: [`../../../../../docs/prd/P15-workflow-wiring-optimization.md`](../../../../../docs/prd/P15-workflow-wiring-optimization.md)
§6 (FR22–FR30), §7. Design reasoning: [`../../design.md`](../../design.md) Decision 10.
**Shared contract:** [`../../../p13-prompt-model-optimization/specs/authored-change/spec.md`](../../../p13-prompt-model-optimization/specs/authored-change/spec.md)
— one spine two origins, origin-blind refusals, preflight, `unverified` labeling, conflicts, reversal,
audit, entitlement, offline parity, no-new-egress, and *a user may not author the evidence*. Every
requirement there applies here and is **not** restated.

Covers the user-initiated half of the structural axis: reordering, parallelizing, merging and pruning
nodes directly, instead of waiting for `OpReorder`, `OpMerge` or `OpPrune` to propose it.

> **Why this is the most dangerous authoring surface in the product.** A graph editor *looks* like it can
> do anything. Dragging a node is a two-second gesture that expresses an arbitrary rewiring, and nothing
> about the gesture communicates that the platform can materialize exactly **one** shape — a transposition
> of two adjacent, independent statements — while a merge, a prune, an edge change, or a non-adjacent move
> is refused at transform. Left unaddressed, the editor becomes a machine for generating disappointment:
> the user rearranges their workflow, the platform accepts it, and then nothing can be applied.
>
> Worse, an unmaterializable wiring change is not even **scoreable**. A wiring-changed `config_hash`
> evaluated against unchanged source would be scored as the base configuration under a different hash —
> a false result, and the exact failure the interim refusal exists to prevent. So this capability holds a
> harder line than the other three axes: an authored wiring change the transform cannot materialize is
> not merely un-appliable, it must **not be presented as a scoreable variant**, and the surface must say
> which shape it was and why.
>
> The compensating half is that this axis has the strongest *pre-submission* safety net anywhere in the
> platform. The typed-contract coherence gate can decide, before a single line of codemod is generated,
> whether a proposed ordering breaks a producer→consumer contract — and when a catalogued adapter can
> reconcile it, the adapter is an **explicit inserted node the user sees in the preview**, never a hidden
> runtime coercion. Moving that gate to preflight turns the graph editor from a guessing surface into one
> that tells the truth about every gesture as it is made.

## ADDED Requirements

### Requirement: A user SHALL be able to author a wiring change, and the coherence gate SHALL run at preflight

An authoring surface SHALL let a user reorder nodes, mark data-independent nodes parallelizable, merge
two adjacent nodes, and prune a node. Every authored wiring draft SHALL be validated by the typed-contract
coherence gate **before** submission and before any codemod is generated.

#### Scenario: A wiring draft is gated before submission

- **WHEN** a user drafts a reordering of a workflow's nodes
- **THEN** the coherence gate evaluates the draft at preflight
- **AND** no codemod, diff, or pull request is generated by the evaluation.

#### Scenario: Every authored wiring change derives, never mutates

- **WHEN** an authored wiring change is submitted
- **THEN** the resulting variant carries the parent's identifier as its lineage
- **AND** the parent's ordering and edges are unchanged
- **AND** because ordering and edges are identity-bearing, the resulting configuration hash differs from the parent's.

### Requirement: An authored ordering that violates a typed I/O contract SHALL be refused at preflight, naming the producer, the consumer, and the field

An authored ordering or rewiring that leaves a consumer's required input undefined SHALL be refused before
submission. The refusal SHALL name the consuming node, the producing node whose output it required, and
the field that would become undefined.

#### Scenario: A move that orphans a consumer's input is refused with all three names

- **WHEN** a user moves a node ahead of the predecessor that produces a field it consumes
- **THEN** preflight returns `refused`
- **AND** the refusal names the consumer, the producer, and the field
- **AND** no runnable spec, diff, or pull request is produced.

#### Scenario: The refusal is identical to the operator path's

- **WHEN** the same contract-violating ordering arises from an operator candidate and from an authored draft
- **THEN** both are rejected by the same gate with the same typed cause.

### Requirement: An adapter that reconciles an authored reordering SHALL be shown as an explicit node before submission

Where a catalogued adapter reconciles a producer→consumer mismatch in an authored draft, the ordering
SHALL be admitted as adapted, and the adapter SHALL be presented to the user as an **explicit inserted
node** in the preview, with its edges rewired producer→adapter→consumer, before the user submits. The
adapter SHALL ship as generated source in the same reviewable diff and SHALL NOT be a hidden runtime
coercion.

#### Scenario: The user sees the adapter before agreeing to it

- **WHEN** an authored reordering is admissible only because a catalogued adapter reconciles a mismatch
- **THEN** preflight reports the ordering as adapted
- **AND** the inserted adapter node and its rewired edges are shown in the preview
- **AND** the user's submission is of a draft that visibly contains the adapter.

#### Scenario: An adapter that would drop a required field is not offered

- **WHEN** no catalogued adapter can reconcile the mismatch without dropping something the consumer requires
- **THEN** the ordering is refused rather than adapted
- **AND** no adapter is inserted.

#### Scenario: Adapter identity is deterministic

- **WHEN** the same authored reordering is preflighted twice
- **THEN** the same adapters are inserted
- **AND** the resulting configuration hash is identical.

### Requirement: An authored wiring change whose shape the transform cannot materialize SHALL be refused at preflight, naming the shape

An authored wiring change SHALL be admissible for application only where the transform can materialize it.
Every other shape — a merge, a prune, an edge change, a non-adjacent move, or more than one transposition
at once — SHALL be refused at preflight with the **shape** named, and the surface SHALL NOT present it as
an applicable change.

#### Scenario: A merge is refused with its shape named

- **WHEN** a user drafts a merge of two adjacent nodes
- **THEN** preflight returns `refused`, naming the shape as a merge and stating that no materializer exists for it
- **AND** the surface does not offer the draft as an applicable change.

#### Scenario: An adjacent independent transposition is admissible

- **WHEN** a user transposes two adjacent, independent sibling statements in a language with a statement materializer
- **THEN** preflight returns `admissible`
- **AND** applying it produces a diff that is a permutation of the file's lines.

#### Scenario: Unprovable independence is a refusal, not an attempt

- **WHEN** a user transposes two adjacent statements whose independence the analysis cannot prove
- **THEN** preflight returns `refused`, naming the reason
- **AND** no diff is produced.

#### Scenario: A language without a statement materializer refuses by name

- **WHEN** a user drafts a transposition in a language with no statement materializer
- **THEN** preflight returns `refused`, naming the language
- **AND** the language boundary is stated rather than the control being silently unavailable.

### Requirement: An unmaterializable authored wiring change SHALL NOT be presented as a scoreable variant

A wiring change the transform refuses SHALL NOT be offered for evaluation, SHALL NOT be enqueued as an
evaluation run, and SHALL NOT be displayed as a variant awaiting a score. Where the surface retains such a
draft, it SHALL retain it as a **recorded intent** explicitly marked as neither applicable nor scoreable.

#### Scenario: A refused wiring draft cannot be evaluated

- **WHEN** a user requests evaluation of an authored wiring draft the transform refuses
- **THEN** the request is refused with the shape named
- **AND** no evaluation run is enqueued
- **AND** no configuration hash for that draft is submitted for scoring.

#### Scenario: A recorded intent is not a variant

- **WHEN** a refused wiring draft is retained by the surface
- **THEN** it is labeled as a recorded intent that cannot currently be applied or scored
- **AND** it does not appear among the workflow's scoreable variants.

### Requirement: An authored parallelization SHALL be refused unless the nodes are provably data-independent

An authored change marking two nodes parallelizable SHALL be admissible only where the analysis proves the
nodes are data-independent. Where independence cannot be proven, the change SHALL be refused naming the
dependency that blocks it.

#### Scenario: A dependency blocks parallelization by name

- **WHEN** a user marks two nodes parallelizable and one consumes a field the other produces
- **THEN** preflight returns `refused`, naming the dependency
- **AND** no diff is produced.

### Requirement: An authored wiring change SHALL be applicable while unverified and SHALL NOT be reported as a structural improvement

A user MAY apply an authored wiring change the transform can materialize without requesting verification.
Such a change SHALL be `unverified`, and no latency, cost, or quality benefit SHALL be attributed to it
until the harness has run.

#### Scenario: An authored transposition applies without a claim

- **WHEN** a user applies a materializable authored transposition without requesting verification
- **THEN** a diff is produced
- **AND** the change is `unverified`
- **AND** no latency, token, or quality benefit is attributed to it.

### Requirement: A wiring refusal SHALL distinguish a broken graph from an unapplicable shape

A refusal because the ordering violates a typed I/O contract and a refusal because the transform cannot
materialize the shape SHALL be presented as different answers with different next steps. The first SHALL
name the consumer, the producer, and the field and SHALL invite a correction; the second SHALL name the
shape and SHALL state that the limitation is the platform's.

#### Scenario: A broken graph names the break and invites a fix

- **WHEN** an authored ordering runs a consumer before its producer
- **THEN** the refusal names the consuming node, the producing node, and the field
- **AND** it offers a next step, such as restoring the order or supplying the field from elsewhere.

#### Scenario: An unapplicable shape names the shape and owns the limitation

- **WHEN** an authored merge, prune, edge change, non-adjacent move, or multi-swap is refused
- **THEN** the refusal names the shape
- **AND** it states that no rewriter emits that shape yet, rather than implying the graph is wrong.

#### Scenario: The two are never worded the same

- **WHEN** both refusals are rendered
- **THEN** neither can be mistaken for the other
- **AND** a reader can tell whether to change their graph or to wait for the platform.

### Requirement: A retained refused wiring draft SHALL be described as a recorded intent, never as pending

Where a surface retains a refused wiring draft, it SHALL describe it as retained, not applicable, and not
scored. It SHALL NOT describe it as pending, queued, awaiting evaluation, or scheduled, and it SHALL NOT
display it among the workflow's variants.

#### Scenario: A recorded intent is not described as work in progress

- **WHEN** a refused wiring draft is displayed
- **THEN** it is described as retained and not applicable
- **AND** the words pending, queued, awaiting, and scheduled do not describe its state.

#### Scenario: A recorded intent carries the reason it cannot be applied

- **WHEN** a recorded intent is displayed
- **THEN** the reason it was refused is shown with it
- **AND** the reason is the engine's own, rendered verbatim.
